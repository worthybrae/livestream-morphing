# A first selection

The common movement is the identity of the collection. The best companions change the physical character of the image enough that the same distortion feels newly expressive.

**Graphite weather** is the clearest departure from the live piece: pale paper, open street, and restrained hatching. It is a good opening work.

**Black river** gives the collection weight. The crossing becomes a set of carved openings, and the broad dark field makes the familiar movement more legible.

**A street, woven** is the most unusual material experiment. Its fixed threads bend with the same wave field. It belongs beside the drawings because the street remains recognizable, but the surface feels constructed differently.

**Blue hour** and **Phosphor garden** stay close to the live work. They are useful quieter companions between more graphic pieces.

**Red registration** introduces restrained color without abandoning the line drawing. Its fine screen rewards a large view; it should not be judged only from the small thumbnail.

**Mineral morning** changes the balance from lines toward flat shapes. It is the most natural bridge toward a separate series of color works.

**Street pressure** is the most restless study. Brightness bands make the scene feel mapped, but they also expose foliage and small changes in illumination. Keep it when you want density, not as the default representation of the whole collection.

The remaining nine studies are alternatives for further curation. In particular, the stippled and halftone films contain much more fine texture and produce substantially larger video files. They are local experiments, not recommendations for an always-on stream.

For a first public presentation, use the original live artwork as the main piece and choose three or four contrasting companions from this shortlist. The full 17-study viewing room is an artist's selection tool.
